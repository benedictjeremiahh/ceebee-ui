# CashFlowChart <span className="docs__label">Composition</span>

Money in and out per period, with the running balance over them. `BalanceCurve` shows *that* cash dips;
this shows *why* — which week's bills pull it under and which receipt brings it back. It is the shape
cash-forecast dashboards converge on: inflow bars up, outflow bars down, the balance as a line across both.

## Start from the real balance

`opening` is the balance before the first period — today's cash for a forecast, or the opening of a historical window. The line starts there and runs through
each period's close. Starting from zero draws a projection of *change*, which reads like a projection of
cash and is not one.

## What it states

Above the plot, one sentence gives the two figures a decision needs: the first period below the
threshold and the lowest balance. The period selector marks a dip, including one hidden inside a period
that later closes above the threshold. A dot marks the lowest point on the plot; the sentence supplies
its exact figure. Lightweight Charts draws the bars, line, threshold, grid and numeric axis. Hover a
period on the plot to read its exact inflow, outflow and closing balance without interpreting the
compact axis labels. If the period's intra-period low differs from its close, the detail shows that too.

## Periods are the consumer's

The chart does not bucket. Pass days, weeks or months, each with its `start` day, `inflow` and `outflow`
(positive; a negative outflow is read as the same amount). A period bucketed from finer figures can dip and recover
inside itself — a week that is overdrawn on Wednesday and paid on Friday closes above zero. Pass its `low`
(the lowest balance within it) and the chart reads that against the threshold, marks its period and plots
the dip, so a mid-period shortfall is never hidden by the close. `formatPeriod` names a period on the
chart's axis and in the selector; by default it is its start day in the document's language. The
axis chooses which labels fit at the available width. An optional signed `adjustment` changes the running
balance without creating an inflow/outflow bar. It appears separately in exact details and the table;
use `adjustmentLabel` to name it. Never disguise a balance adjustment as a receipt or payment.

## Interaction contract

Read-only unless `onSelectPeriod` is given. Then each period is a button named with its date and all three
exact figures, `aria-pressed` for `selectedPeriod`, so the consumer can list what is behind it beside the chart.
Keyboard focus on any period reveals the same exact-value detail as hover. Without an action callback,
periods remain focusable labelled images, not buttons that promise an unavailable action.
The detail follows the active period near the pointer, or its balance point during keyboard focus.
It flips and stays within the chart bounds near an edge rather than jumping to the opposite chart edge.
Pass `periodControlsLabel` to put the exact-value table in an initially closed native disclosure.
This leaves only the plot's date axis visible in a compact overview. Enter or Space on the summary
opens rows with the period, inflow, outflow, optional adjustment and closing balance. Numbers use
`formatExact`, not the abbreviated axis format. Read-only period labels are plain row headings;
when `onSelectPeriod` is provided they retain their selection buttons and keyboard detail.
Use `periodLabel` to translate the period column heading. Do not label a date-only selector as an
exact-amount table.
For a read-only overview where hover details are sufficient, omit `periodControlsLabel` and pass
`compact`. Period controls stay visually hidden until keyboard focus, while exact values remain
available in the screen-reader table. Do not add a second visible table merely to repeat the plot.

## Accessibility

A visually hidden table gives every period's in, out and balance by default. With `periodControlsLabel`,
the same table is visible when the disclosure is opened; there is no duplicate hidden table or date
selector. The canvas is `aria-hidden` because the table is the exact accessible rendering. Under
`forced-colors: active` the canvas is replaced by an always-visible table, even with a disclosure label.
Pass `formatExact` when `format` abbreviates the axis; the library has no currency
assumption. The chart has no entry animation, including under reduced motion.

## Tokens

`--cb-tone-success` fills inflows, `--cb-tone-danger` fills outflows and marks a period below the
threshold, `--cb-tone-brand` draws the balance and low point, `--cb-border` draws the grid and selector
separator, `--cb-fg-muted` the axis text, `--cb-focus-width` the focus ring, and the existing
`--cb-radius-*`, `--cb-space-*`, and `--cb-text-*` Tokens size the component.
