# ProgressCurve <span className="docs__label">Composition</span>

An S-curve: what a plan said would be complete by each day, against what was reported. It answers one
question — *how far behind are we, today, in points* — and says the answer in words before it draws
anything.

## Reading, then picture

The sentence above the chart is not a caption. It is the reading on the day the reader is standing
on: the actual, the planned, and the gap between them in points. Somebody checking a job on a phone
between sites gets their answer without interpreting a line, and the picture is there for the shape
of the slip — when it started, whether it is still widening.

A day nobody reported is **not** a day of no progress. Both series carry the last report forward, so
a gap in reporting reads as a flat stretch rather than a collapse to zero, which would say the work
went backwards.

The vertical scale is pinned to 0–100%. Autoscaling would stretch a job that has reached 30% to the
full height of the plot, which reads as *nearly there* — the opposite of the truth.

## Interaction contract

Read-only. A crosshair follows the pointer; there is no pan, no zoom, no selection and no dismissal.
A dashboard chart is read, not traded, and panning would let a reader scroll the plan off the screen
with no way back.

What is marked on the plot is the **last report**, not today. A marker attaches to a data point, and
today is usually not one — a "today" mark snapped to whichever reported day sat nearest it and then
carried a label that was false, an arrow reading *Today* pointing four days off it. The last reported
day is a point that always exists, so the arrow lands exactly on it, and it tells a reader the one
thing the line cannot: how stale the actual is. Today's reading is stated in words above the plot,
where it needs no pixel to land on.

## Accessibility

The plot is a `<canvas>`, so it is not in the accessibility tree at all. This component therefore
ships a second rendering rather than a description of the first: a table of every day either series
reports, with both values and the gap. It is always in the DOM, visually hidden, and built from the
same numbers as the line.

Under `forced-colors: active` the canvas is **not rendered at all** and the table takes its place. A
bitmap is the one thing a forced-colors user cannot have recoloured for them, and an empty labelled
region holding a chart's worth of blank space is a worse answer than the readings themselves.

Colour is never the only difference between the two lines: the plan is dashed and thin, the actual is
solid and thick, in the plot and in the legend.

## Substrate

The canvas, the Token reading, the forced-colors path and the accessible table belong to
**TimeSeriesChart**, the primitive every dated chart here is built from. This Composition adds the part
that is about progress: the scale pinned to 0–100%, the two series with their dash treatment, and the
reading in words.

That split happened when the second dated chart arrived
([BalanceCurve](/data-display/balance-curve)). The shared half is ~200 lines of canvas and Token
handling in which three defects had already been found — a theme applied during the substrate's async
import being lost for good, an invisible table-sized box lying across the plot, and a mark labelled
"Today" pointing four days off it. A second copy would have carried whichever two were fixed last.

Underneath, TradingView's [Lightweight Charts](https://github.com/tradingview/lightweight-charts) 5.2.1
(Apache-2.0), **imported dynamically inside the mount effect**. A server render never evaluates it, and
a consumer who shows no chart never downloads it — in a real consumer's production build it lands as its
own 57 KB gzipped chunk. Attribution lives in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` rather than on the canvas.

Tokens are read from the DOM with `getComputedStyle` and re-read whenever the theme, the Skin or the
system colour scheme changes — a canvas cannot inherit CSS. Where they do not resolve, nothing is drawn:
the component will not invent a colour, and the table stands as the rendering until they do.

## Tokens

`--cb-tone-brand` (actual), `--cb-fg-subtle` (planned, crosshair), `--cb-fg-muted`, `--cb-fg`,
`--cb-border` (grid and axes), `--cb-surface` (plot ground), `--cb-font-sans`, `--cb-tone-danger`
and `--cb-tone-success` for the reading, plus `--cb-space-*`, `--cb-text-*` and `--cb-radius-*`.
