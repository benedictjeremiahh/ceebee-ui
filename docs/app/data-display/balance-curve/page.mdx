# BalanceCurve <span className="docs__label">Composition</span>

A running balance over days, read against a line: a cash projection, a budget remaining, a tank. It
answers two questions a list of days cannot — **when does this go under, and how far down does it get.**

## The line is the argument

The plot is drawn as a **baseline** series, not a line: it shades above and below the threshold in the
semantic colours, so the stretch in trouble is coloured by its own values. That replaces the usual
treatment — a red badge on every bad row — which says nothing about depth. Seven identical warnings and
a dip of one percent look the same; a shaded area does not.

The threshold defaults to zero and is deliberately a prop. A business that must keep a minimum on hand
is in trouble well before it reaches nothing, and a chart drawn against zero tells it everything is
fine right up to the day it is not.

<BalanceCurveBufferDemo />

Above the plot, the reading in words: the day it goes under and the lowest it gets. A chart shows the
shape; the sentence gives the two figures a decision needs, so nobody reads a position off an axis to
act on it. "Below" is strict — a balance sitting exactly on the threshold has met it, and reporting
that as a breach would cry wolf on the one day the business did precisely what it planned.

## No currency

The library holds no currency and no locale. `format` is a function the product passes, so the same
chart serves rupiah, a percentage of a budget, or litres of diesel — and the compact form a dashboard
wants stays the product's decision, not this component's.

## Interaction contract

Read-only. A crosshair follows the pointer; there is no pan, no zoom, no selection and no dismissal —
the same contract as [ProgressCurve](/data-display/progress-curve), because both are read rather than
explored. The mark on the plot is the **low point**, which is a day the series definitely reports, so
the arrow lands exactly on it.

## Accessibility

The plot is a `<canvas>`, so it is not in the accessibility tree at all. Like every dated chart here it
ships a second rendering rather than a description of the first: a table of every day, always in the
DOM, visually hidden, built from the same numbers. Under `forced-colors: active` the canvas is **not
rendered** and the table takes its place.

## Tokens

`--cb-tone-success` and `--cb-tone-danger` shade above and below the threshold, `--cb-tone-brand` the
line, `--cb-fg-muted` and `--cb-fg-subtle` the text and crosshair, `--cb-border` the grid and axes,
`--cb-surface` the plot ground, `--cb-font-sans`, plus `--cb-space-*`, `--cb-text-*` and
`--cb-radius-*`.
